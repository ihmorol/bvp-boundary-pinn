Architecture: what goes in and what comes out

Input: one coordinate eta. The complete physical case is fixed outside the network.
↓
One smooth network: five outputs f, g, theta, phi, chi. Automatic differentiation gives their eta derivatives, including four for f.
↓
Interior calculation: substitute predictions and derivatives into the five original equations. One error per equation per point.
Boundary calculation: evaluate at zero and L. Calculate all twelve original value, slope and coupled errors.
↓
One loss: scale, square, average equation errors, and add all weighted boundary errors. Backpropagation updates only the NN weights.

The loss, rendered

J=∑i=15λi1N∑n=1N[Ri(ηn)si]2+∑j=112μj[Bjtj]2

Read aloud: J equals the sum of five weighted average squared equation errors, divided by their scales, plus the sum of twelve weighted squared boundary errors, divided by their scales.

N is the number of interior positions. R is an original equation error; B is an original boundary error. Lambda and mu are positive training weights; s and t are fixed scales. Squaring applies after dividing by each scale. These are not additional physical parameters.

A defensible research contribution

Our proposed question is whether a tested combination of sampling, loss scaling and derivative representation can solve this specific five-field (4,2,2,2,2) problem reliably, including the requested near-boundary coefficients.

A useful result could be a reproducible benchmark with a converged reference, corrected source transcription, per-condition error reporting, boundary-sensitive accuracy, measured cost and controlled method comparisons. If a tested change improves these, report the measured improvement for the tested parameter cases.

Do not claim “first five-field PINN,” “new best optimizer,” guaranteed stability, uniqueness or a speed advantage before a broader novelty search and the experiments. A plain PINN applied to another model does not by itself establish a new algorithm.

One training step

Text version of the architecture diagram.

flowchart TD
  I[Training points eta, N by 1] --> X[Log input map]
  X --> NN[tanh network 40 x 4, five outputs]
  ST[Smooth start profile, fixed] --> NN
  NN --> BL[Boundary layer: B1 to B12 exact for any weights]
  P[Locked coefficients] --> BL
  BL --> J[Taylor jets: f to order 4, g theta phi chi to order 2]
  J --> R[R14 to R18, original equations]
  P --> R
  R --> S[Fixed scale s of eta, from eta and the locked values]
  S --> L[One scalar loss: mean of scaled squares]
  L --> FIN{Finite?}
  FIN -->|No| FAIL[Stop and save the failure]
  FIN -->|Yes| G[Backward pass to the network weights]
  G --> OPT[Adam, then L-BFGS and Levenberg-Marquardt with weights frozen]
  OPT -.->|new weights| NN

The locked coefficients are never in the optimizer's parameter list. In exact mode the boundary conditions hold by construction, so the loss holds only the equations.
